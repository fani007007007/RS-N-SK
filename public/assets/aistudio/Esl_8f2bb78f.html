🍽️ Vocabulary & Word Formation — Partitives, Families & Phonics

This topic teaches three connected systems: container words, animal family names, and vowel spelling patterns. Master the system, not just the list.

1A · Partitive (Unit) Nouns — "a ___ of ___"

Liquids, grains, powders, and many foods are mass nouns. To count them, English uses a partitive: the container, shape, portion, or vessel.

TypeExamplesLogic
Container sold incarton of milk, jar of jam, can of soup, bottle of water, bag of riceNamed for packaging.
Shape made inloaf of bread, bar of chocolate, slice of cake, stick of butterNamed for form.
Small piecegrain of sand, flake of snow, drop of rain, pinch of saltNamed for portion.
Serving vesselcup of tea, bowl of cereal, glass of water, plate of pastaNamed for what you eat/drink from.
Natural unithead of cabbage, clove of garlic, ear of corn, bunch of grapesNamed for how it grows.
Real-world facts: The waxed-paper Tetra Pak carton was invented in 1951 by Swedish engineer Erik Wallenberg. Metal cans were patented by Peter Durand around 1810, and Nicolas Appert pioneered glass-jar preservation in France. The Mason jar was patented in 1858. The word loaf comes from Old English hlāf.

Magic 'e': In vowel + consonant + e, the final e is silent but makes the earlier vowel long: hop → hope, bit → bite, cut → cute. This is a spelling fossil from Old English, when the final e was once pronounced.

🔤 Countable & Uncountable Nouns — the complete system

Count nouns are separate objects: one egg, two eggs. Uncount nouns are masses, liquids, powders, abstract ideas, or categories: water, rice, information, music.

TestCountableUncountable
a / an✅ an egg❌ ~~a water~~
plural -s✅ two tomatoes❌ ~~two breads~~
many / much✅ many apples✅ much water
a few / a little✅ a few carrots✅ a little cheese
verb✅ The eggs are fresh✅ The meat is fresh

Seven families of uncountable nouns

  1. Liquids: water, milk, juice, tea, coffee, oil.
  2. Powders/grains: rice, sugar, salt, flour, pepper, sand.
  3. Materials: wood, metal, plastic, glass, paper, wool.
  4. Foods as masses: bread, meat, cheese, butter, fish, fruit.
  5. Abstract ideas: information, advice, music, fun, homework, progress, research, knowledge.
  6. School subjects/languages: English, maths, physics, economics, news.
  7. Meals/weather/games: breakfast, lunch, weather, rain, chess, football, swimming, luggage, furniture, equipment, money.
Memorise these: information, advice, homework, furniture, luggage, equipment, machinery, jewellery, clothing, money, news, bread, meat, cheese, rice, sugar, weather, research, evidence, knowledge, progress, fun, music, transport, scenery.

⚖️ Comparative Adjectives — compare accurately

Comparative adjectives show the difference between two things. We usually use than: The red car is faster than the blue car.

The Golden Rules

RuleChangeExamples
1 syllableadd -erold → older, tall → taller, fast → faster
Ends in eadd -rlarge → larger, nice → nicer, wise → wiser
CVC endingdouble final consonant + erbig → bigger, hot → hotter, thin → thinner
2 syllables ending yy → i + erhappy → happier, easy → easier, busy → busier
2+ syllables not yuse moremore beautiful, more expensive, more interesting

Irregular comparatives

BaseComparative
goodbetter
badworse
farfarther / further
littleless
many/muchmore

Four comparison types

  1. Inequality: A cheetah is faster than a lion.
  2. Equality: use as + adjective + as: My coffee is as hot as yours.
  3. Progressive change: comparative + and + comparative: The weather is getting colder and colder.
  4. Correlative / parallel: The + comparative, the + comparative: The harder you work, the luckier you get.
Common errors: "more better" ❌ → "better" ✅; "hoter" ❌ → "hotter" ✅; "prettier that" ❌ → "prettier than" ✅.

Riddles

Riddle 1: I am colder than ice, but harder than rock. I am smaller than a planet, but bigger than a marble. What am I?
Answer: A snowball or hailstone.
Riddle 2: I am faster than a walk, but slower than a sprint. What am I?
Answer: A jog.
Riddle 3: I am more expensive than a book, but cheaper than a car. I am lighter than a laptop, but heavier than a phone. What am I?
Answer: A tablet.

Authentic facts: Blue whales are the largest animals ever known, up to about 30 m and 150+ tonnes. Their hearts can weigh around 180 kg, and their calls are among the loudest animal sounds. Sperm whales dive over 1,000 m; scientific tracking has recorded beaked whales near 3,000 m for over three hours. 📖 Reading & Comprehension — strategies + exam passage

  1. Skimming: get the gist quickly.
  2. Scanning: find a specific detail.
  3. Literal: answer is in the text.
  4. Inference: text clue + your reasoning.
🧱 PEEL: Point, Evidence, Explain, Link. For 4-mark inference, use 2–3 sentences. For 8-mark opinion, use 4–5 sentences, a describer, a reason, and a link back to the text.

A Memorable Day at the Zoo

Last Sunday, my family went to the City Zoo. It was a sunny day, perfect for a picnic. We packed a loaf of bread, a carton of juice, and some apples. When we arrived, we saw many animals.

First, we visited the monkey enclosure. The monkeys were monkeying around on the trees, swinging from branch to branch. It was funny to watch them.

Next, we went to the lion's den. The lion looked very fierce, but he was actually sleeping. My little sister was scared, but I told her it was a piece of cake to watch him from behind the glass.

At lunchtime, we were so hungry that we wolfed down our sandwiches. After eating, we felt like couch potatoes, so we sat on a bench and watched the birds. Suddenly, a duck came close to us. My dad tried to feed it, but the duck ducked out and ran away into the pond. It was a memorable meal and a fun day!

QModel answer
1. What food containers are mentioned?A loaf of bread and a carton of juice.
2. What does "wolfed down" mean?They ate their sandwiches extremely quickly.
3. Inference: why couch potatoes?They had eaten a large meal quickly, felt full and lazy, and wanted to sit still and rest, like people watching TV.
4. One uncountable + one countable nounUncountable: juice/bread. Countable: apples/monkey/bird/duck/branch/sandwich.
5. Add an animal and why"I would add an elephant because elephants are huge and gentle. They would be more impressive than the sleeping lion, and my little sister would not be scared because elephants are known for being kind."

✍️ Functional Dialogue & Narrative Writing

Restaurant English

JobPolite formula
Order mainI would like the grilled chicken, please.
Order drinkYes, a glass of orange juice, please.
Ask dessertWould you like any dessert?
RefuseNo, thank you. Just the bill, please.
Mark tips: use please; use a container word for drinks; questions need inversion and a question mark.

Narrative: memorable meal

Use: context → action + idiom → sensory detail → reflection. Keep past tense.

"The most memorable meal I had was my grandmother's birthday dinner last summer. There was so much food that we all pigged out on dumplings and noodles. My cousin wolfed down two slices of cake in under a minute. Afterwards, we turned into complete couch potatoes and watched fireworks from the sofa. I will never forget it because my whole family was laughing together."

Lost marks: no idiom, wrong tense, too few sentences.
